How it works — and what stays private

  1. You listen. Every finished play, skip and like becomes a small signal about artists, genres and decades.
  2. It scores songs locally. A transparent weighting model on your device ranks what to show you — no cloud, no account.
  3. You steer. Rate songs in a training round, boost or block artists, freeze learning, or reset everything.
  4. Nothing leaves the device. The only network traffic is talking to YouTube for songs. No analytics, no ads, no sign-in.

Questions

Is it safe? My phone shows a warning.

Android's Play Protect warns about any app it hasn't seen before — that is "unknown", not "malicious". The app asks for very little: internet, notifications, playback service and (only if you import music) audio files. No contacts, location, camera or SMS. The code is public on GitHub.

Is this official? Is it legal?

TuneBox is an independent, unofficial project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by YouTube or Google. It is a personal-use player; please respect the terms of the services you use.

Does it cost anything or show ads?

No. It is free and open source under the MIT licence, with no ads, no accounts and no analytics.

Something broke — where do I report it?

Open an issue on GitHub. Say which device and version (Settings → About) and what you tapped.
